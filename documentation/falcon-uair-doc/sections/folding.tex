\section{Proof-size optimization: folding the arbitrary-poly lane}\label{sec:folding}
% ===========================================================================

\paragraph{Where the bytes are.}
For this instance the proof is overwhelmingly polynomial-commitment data.
A measured breakdown of an (MLE-first) Falcon proof gives
\emph{$94.4\%$} in the Zip$+$ commitment-opening bytes
(\texttt{proof.zip}); the ideal check ($2.0\%$), the combined-poly resolver
($1.1\%$), the inner sumcheck and multipoint evaluation
($<0.1\%$ each) are negligible. Within \texttt{proof.zip}, the binary lane
is \emph{empty} (Falcon has no binary columns) and the integer lane is a
single $\col{slack}$ column, so the bytes are essentially \emph{all}
arbitrary-poly: the $112$ committed arb columns of \cref{sec:layout}.
Shrinking the proof therefore means shrinking the arbitrary-poly opening.

\paragraph{Degree folding.}
The Zip$+$ PCS supports \emph{column splitting} along the cell-degree axis,
the workhorse of its folded variants. A degree-$<W$ cell
$v = u + X^{W/2} w$ splits into low/high halves $u, w$ of degree $<W/2$;
applying this twice ($W \to W/2 \to W/4$) turns a length-$n$ column of
degree-$<W$ cells into a length-$4n$ column of degree-$<W/4$ cells, gaining
two multilinear variables that index the four degree-quarters. Committing
the quarter-degree column makes every codeword element a quarter of the
size, so the column openings shrink correspondingly. The bridge from the
PIOP's evaluation claim on the original column $v$ at point $r_0$ to a PCS
claim on the split column is a pair of Fiat--Shamir folding challenges
$\gamma_1, \gamma_2$: the prover opens the split column at the
\emph{extended point} $(r_0 \,\Vert\, \gamma_1 \,\Vert\, \gamma_2)$, and the
verifier reassembles the four quarter-evaluations $c_{00}, c_{10}, c_{01},
c_{11}$ (the inner products of the quarter-blocks of the lifted evaluation
with the code's powers) by the bilinear corner weights
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fourblock}
  (1-\gamma_1)(1-\gamma_2)\,c_{00}
  + \gamma_1(1-\gamma_2)\,c_{10}
  + (1-\gamma_1)\gamma_2\,c_{01}
  + \gamma_1\gamma_2\,c_{11}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Why a dedicated arb fold (and not the generic 4$\times$ fold).}
A Zip$+$ column opening costs (coefficient width) $\times$ (cell degree).
The generic $4\times$ folded path couples the arbitrary-poly coefficient
width to the integer lane, which the $4\times$ integer split forces to
$\ge 4$ machine limbs. Folding Falcon's arb cells \emph{through that path}
would widen each $\approx 30$-bit coefficient to $256$ bits: cell size
$1{\times}W$ becomes $4{\times}(W/4)$, and the $4\times$ degree win is
exactly cancelled by the $4\times$ width bloat --- net zero. Falcon's arb
coefficients are narrow, so we use a \emph{dedicated} fold that splits
\emph{only} the arbitrary-poly lane along degree while keeping the native
narrow ($64$-bit) coefficient type, and leaves the (empty) binary and
(tiny) integer lanes unfolded. Concretely the arb lane commits and opens at
the extended point $(r_0\,\Vert\,\gamma_1\,\Vert\,\gamma_2)$ with the
quarter-degree reassembly \eqref{eq:fourblock}, while the integer lane
commits and opens unsplit at $r_0$; since the two lanes open at different
points they are committed as separate Merkle instances.

\paragraph{Result.}
The degree fold reduces the arbitrary-poly opening --- the $94\%$
component --- by close to $4\times$. End-to-end (Falcon-512, $2^4$
signatures, identical trace) the proof shrinks from
\(\approx 6.37\,\mathrm{MB}\) to \(\approx 1.77\,\mathrm{MB}\), a
\(\mathbf{3.6\times}\) reduction, at essentially unchanged prover time
($\approx 45\,$ms with the MLE-first lane) and a negligible increase in
verifier time. The optimization is orthogonal to the arithmetization of
\cref{sec:arith,sec:norm}: it composes with whichever ideal-check lane is
used.
