\section{The algorithm}
\label{sec:algorithm}

\subsection{Field and ring choices}
\label{sec:field-choices}

Fix
\[
  f(X) \;=\; X^{32} + X^7 + X^3 + X^2 + 1 \;\in\; \F_2[X],
\]
an irreducible polynomial of degree~$32$ (the AES-style choice). Define
$\GF(2^{32}) = \F_2[X]/f$ and $\flift \in \Z[X]$ to be the integer
polynomial with the same set of monomials and the same $\{0,1\}$
coefficients. The lifted ring is
\[
  \Rlift \;=\; \Z[X]/\flift,
\]
a $32$-dimensional $\Z$-module on which addition is component-wise and
multiplication is schoolbook poly-multiplication followed by reduction
modulo $\flift$. The reduction is simply
\[
  X^{32} \;\equiv\; X^7 + X^3 + X^2 + 1 \pmod{\flift},
\]
so any product whose temporary degree exceeds $31$ is folded back by
contributing to four low positions per high monomial.

\subsection{The Cantor basis}
\label{sec:cantor}

The Cantor basis of $\GF(2^{32})$ is the sequence
$\bb_0, \bb_1, \ldots, \bb_{31}$ defined recursively by
\begin{equation}
  \bb_0 = 1, \qquad \bb_i^2 + \bb_i = \bb_{i-1} \quad \text{for } i \ge 1.
  \label{eq:cantor-recurrence}
\end{equation}
The recurrence solvable because $\Tr(\bb_{i-1}) = 0$ at every step
(which is straightforward: $\Tr(\bb_i^2 + \bb_i) = \Tr(\bb_i)^2 + \Tr(\bb_i)
= 0$ in $\F_2$). For~$i = 0$, $\Tr(1) = 32 \cdot 1 = 0$ since the
extension degree is even.

The two crucial properties for the additive FFT are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $V_i := \mathrm{span}_{\F_2}(\bb_0, \ldots, \bb_{i-1})$ is a
    chain of $\F_2$-subspaces of $\GF(2^{32})$, with
    $\dim V_i = i$.
  \item The associated subspace polynomial
    \[
      s_i(X) \;=\; \prod_{\alpha \in V_i} (X + \alpha)
    \]
    is $\F_2$-linearised: only monomials of degree $2^j$ for
    $j = 0, \ldots, i$ appear, all coefficients lie in $\F_2$, and
    the leading coefficient is~$1$. The Cantor basis additionally
    guarantees $s_i(\bb_i) = 1$, which simplifies the
    recurrence to
    \begin{equation}
      s_{i+1}(X) \;=\; s_i(X)^2 + s_i(X).
      \label{eq:cantor-subspace-recurrence}
    \end{equation}
\end{itemize}

Crucially for the structural lift: every $s_i$ coefficient is in
$\F_2$, so its lift to $\Z$ is in $\{0, 1\}$. Combined with the fact
that Cantor basis vectors themselves are elements of $\GF(2^{32})$
(hence their lifts have $\{0, 1\}$ coefficients), every twiddle the
FFT uses is a $\{0, 1\}$-coefficient polynomial in $\Rlift$.

\subsection{The LCH novel polynomial basis FFT}
\label{sec:lch}

Let $m = \log_2 N$, where $N$ is a power of $2$ at most $2^{32}$.
Express the input polynomial $P(X)$ of degree~$< N$ in the
\emph{novel polynomial basis}
\[
  X_i(X) \;=\; \prod_{j \in \{0..m-1\}\,:\, i_j = 1} s_j(X),
\]
where $i_j$ is the $j$-th bit of $i$. Then $P = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} c_i \, X_i$,
and the LCH forward FFT maps the coefficient vector
$(c_0, \ldots, c_{N-1})$ to the evaluation vector $(P(v_0), \ldots,
P(v_{N-1}))$ at the points $v_k = \sum_l k_l \bb_l \in V_m$. The FFT
proceeds in $m$ stages indexed from $i = m-1$ down to $0$. At
stage~$i$:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Partition the data into blocks of size $2^{i+1}$. Within
    each block, pair entry~$k$ with entry~$k + 2^i$ for
    $k = 0, \ldots, 2^i - 1$.
  \item Each block has a fixed \emph{block offset}
    \[
      \omega \;=\; \sum_{l > i \,:\, \text{bit }l\text{ of block\_start set}} \bb_l \;\in\; V_m,
    \]
    and a fixed twiddle $t = s_i(\omega)$.
  \item For each pair $(a, b)$:
    \begin{equation}
      a' = a + t \cdot b, \qquad b' = a' + b = a + (t+1) \cdot b.
      \label{eq:butterfly}
    \end{equation}
\end{itemize}

The data is in natural order both at input and output --- no
bit-reversal permutation is required, unlike the multiplicative
Cooley--Tukey FFT.

\subsection{Structural lift}
\label{sec:lift}

The lifted FFT runs the \emph{exact same algorithm} but with
arithmetic in $\Rlift$ rather than $\GF(2^{32})$. The Cantor basis
elements, subspace polynomial coefficients, and all derived
twiddles are computed once in $\GF(2^{32})$ and then \emph{lifted}
coefficient-by-coefficient from $\F_2$ to $\{0, 1\} \subset \Z$.
The inputs are \code{BinaryPoly<32>} elements, whose
$\F_2$-coefficients also lift trivially.

The butterfly in equation~\eqref{eq:butterfly} becomes a sequence of
additions in $\Rlift$ and one $\Rlift$-multiplication by the lifted
twiddle. The multiplication is implemented as a
\emph{sparse shift-and-accumulate}: for every set bit $i$ of the
lifted twiddle, we add $x$ shifted by $i$ positions to an
accumulator (length-$63$ buffer in the unreduced domain), then
fold the buffer back to length~$32$ via the $\flift$ reduction.

The soundness witness we rely on is the following commutative diagram:
let $\pi_2 : \Rlift \to \GF(2^{32})$ be the coefficient-wise mod-$2$
reduction. Then for any input vector $\mathbf c$,
\[
  \pi_2\!\bigl( \mathrm{LiftedFFT}(\mathrm{lift}(\mathbf c)) \bigr)
  \;=\;
  \mathrm{FFT}_{\GF(2^{32})}(\mathbf c).
\]
This is verified empirically by the test
\code{lifted\_fft\_mod\_2\_matches\_gf32\_fft} on randomized inputs.
A direct argument: every step in the lifted FFT is the same as in
the $\GF(2^{32})$ FFT, but each addition is in $\Z$ rather than
$\F_2$; mod-$2$ reduction is a ring homomorphism that commutes with
both addition and the $\flift$ reduction (since
$f \equiv \flift \pmod 2$).
