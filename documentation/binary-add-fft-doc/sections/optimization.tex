\section{Optimization journey}
\label{sec:optimization}

The first working implementation produced a per-commit cost in the
multi-second range. The following series of optimizations brought
it down to $\sim 232$\,ms at \code{poly\_size = $2^{14}$}, \code{batch = 11}
on Apple~M4 with rayon parallelism enabled. We document each step
because the relative win sizes are a useful guide for similar
codes.

\subsection{Coefficient-width shrink: $\Int<5> \to \Int<2>$}
\label{sec:width-shrink-int}

The initial \code{ZipTypes} used \code{Cw = DensePolynomial<Int<5>, 32>}
(320-bit coefficients) and \code{CombR = DensePolynomial<Int<8>, 32>}
(512-bit). Bit-budget analysis: each butterfly stage can grow
max-abs by a factor of at most $\sim 33$ (the loose
\code{D + 1 = 33} bound), so after $m$ stages the worst case is
$33^m$. For \code{row\_len $\le 2^{16}$} ($m \le 18$):
$33^{18} \approx 2^{91}$, which fits comfortably in \code{Int<2>}
(128~bits) with headroom. \code{Int<5>} and \code{Int<8>} were
wildly over-budget.

Shrinking to \code{Int<2>} / \code{Int<3>} cut per-butterfly cost
by roughly $2.5\times$ (the \code{crypto-bigint} multi-limb add is
dominated by per-limb carry propagation) and reduced Merkle-leaf
sizes correspondingly. \textbf{Speedup: $\sim 1.8\times$.}

\subsection{Zero-twiddle fast path}

For each FFT stage the first block has $\code{block\_offset} = 0$,
and $s_i$ vanishes on $V_i$, so the twiddle is~$0$. The butterfly
then degenerates to $(a, b) \mapsto (a, a+b)$ and the
\code{mul\_by\_lifted\_gf32} call (which is the dominant cost per
butterfly) is skipped. The savings are small in aggregate ($1/N$
of blocks per stage), but free correctness-wise.

\subsection{Generic kernel + native primitive types ($\Int<2> \to i128 \to i32$)}
\label{sec:width-shrink-native}

We refactored \code{mul\_by\_lifted\_gf32},
\code{reduce\_mod\_ftilde}, and \code{add\_in\_z} to be generic
over a \code{FftCoeff} trait
\[
  \code{FftCoeff} \;\equiv\;
    \code{Copy} + \code{ConstZero} + \code{Add<Self, Output=Self>}
    + \code{PartialEq}.
\]
Both \code{Int<N>} and the primitive \code{i64}, \code{i128},
\code{i32} satisfy these bounds, so we can specialize the
\code{FftRingElement} impl on
\code{DensePolynomial<i128, 32>} (or \code{i32}, etc.) to use
native CPU arithmetic, avoiding \code{crypto-bigint}'s generic
multi-limb overhead.

Sequence of width shrinks, each on the same workload
(\code{poly\_size = $2^{14}$, batch = 11}, with rayon):
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
  \toprule
  Coefficient type & Per-coeff bytes & Commit time \\
  \midrule
  \code{Int<5>}                       & 40 & $\sim$\,6\,s (extrapolated) \\
  \code{Int<2>}                       & 16 & 1.71\,s \\
  \code{i128}                         & 16 & 1.26\,s \\
  \code{Int<2>} + \code{parallel}     & 16 & 365\,ms \\
  \code{i128}  + \code{parallel}      & 16 & 265\,ms \\
  \code{i128}  + within-stage par     & 16 & 240\,ms \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The native-\code{i128} step gives roughly $30\%$ over
\code{Int<2>}, which is largely the cost of \code{crypto-bigint}'s
generic limb-by-limb carry propagation versus the CPU's single
\code{adcs} instruction.

\subsection{The {\tt parallel} feature: row-level rayon parallelism}

The biggest single win was enabling the \code{parallel} feature
flag, which routes \code{encode\_rows} through rayon's
\code{cfg\_chunks\_mut!}. At \code{batch = 11} and \code{num\_rows
= 4} per polynomial, there are $44$ independent FFTs that rayon
distributes across CPU cores. \textbf{Speedup: $\sim 4.7\times$.}

\subsection{Within-FFT (stage-level) parallelism}

After row-level parallelism saturates, additional parallelism
within each FFT must be extracted from inside a single row. We
parallelized the block loop within each stage by passing the data
slice through \code{cfg\_chunks\_mut!(data, block\_size)}: each
block's butterflies are independent and operate on a disjoint
sub-slice.

At \code{batch = 11} on Apple~M4 the row-level parallelism already
nearly saturates the CPU, so within-stage parallelism only adds a
modest $\sim 3\%$ on the i128 path. The win would be larger at
smaller batches.

\subsection{Manual NEON SIMD attempt (split-i128 kernel) --- regressed}
\label{sec:simd-attempt}

We attempted a SIMD-friendly variant of \code{mul\_by\_lifted\_gf32}
specialized to $i128$: unpack each $i128$ coefficient into a
separate \code{[u64; 32]} (low limbs) and \code{[i64; 32]} (high
limbs), run the inner loops on $u64$/$i64$ (natively SIMD types),
and collect lo$\to$hi carries in a second pass to break the
data dependency that prevents auto-vectorization of the $i128$
add directly.

The result \emph{regressed by} $\sim 30\%$. The reason: Apple~M4's
NEON has $128$-bit registers (one $i128$ per register), so there
is no per-register parallelism to be had on $i128$ adds; and the
scalar \code{adcs} + \code{add} dual-issue is already at the
throughput ceiling. The split/recombine overhead and the extra
carries scratch array cost more than they save.

The function (\code{mul\_by\_lifted\_gf32\_i128\_split} in
\code{ring\_ops.rs}) is kept in the codebase as \code{\#[allow(dead\_code)]}
with a note pointing at AVX-512 hosts (where 512-bit vectors can
hold 4~$i128$ values and the math may pay).

\subsection{Tightest type: $i128 \to i32$}
\label{sec:i32-shrink}

A direct examination of the codeword as
\[
  P(v_k) \;=\; \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} c_i \cdot X_i(v_k) \quad \text{in } \Rlift
\]
shows that the loose stage-by-stage bound $33^m$ is far too
pessimistic: $P(v_k)$ is a sum of at most $N$ products of
$\{0,1\}$-coefficient elements, each with per-coefficient
magnitude bounded by $\sim 32 \cdot 5 \approx 160$ in $\Rlift$
(the factor of $5$ comes from the $\flift$-reduction fan-in). So
the worst-case magnitude grows \emph{linearly} with $N$, not
exponentially with $m$. At $N = 2^{16}$, worst case is
$\le 2^{16} \cdot 160 \approx 2^{24}$ --- well within $i32$'s
$\pm 2^{31}$ range. The empirical max at $N = 2^{14}$ is around
$2^{21}$ (see \autoref{sec:measurements}).

Switching the bench from $i128$ to $i32$ gave a further
$2.5\times$ commit speedup at the smaller \code{poly\_size = $2^9$}
config (the bench was reconfigured to that workload during the
exercise), since $i32$ uses native $32$-bit ALU ops instead of
$128$-bit, and the per-coefficient storage shrinks $4\times$
(from $16$ to $4$ bytes), with corresponding cache-locality and
Merkle-leaf-size benefits.

\subsection{Summary table}

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
  \toprule
  Step & Speedup at that step & Cumulative \\
  \midrule
  Original (\code{Int<5>}, sequential, naive butterfly) & $1\times$  & $1\times$ \\
  $\Int<5> \to \Int<2>$ + zero-twiddle skip             & $\sim 3.5\times$ & $\sim 3.5\times$ \\
  \code{parallel} feature                               & $\sim 4.7\times$ & $\sim 16\times$  \\
  $\Int<2> \to i128$ (native)                           & $\sim 1.4\times$ & $\sim 23\times$  \\
  Within-stage parallelism                              & $\sim 1.03\times$ & $\sim 24\times$ \\
  Manual NEON SIMD (regressed; reverted)                & $0.7\times$ & --- \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The single biggest win was the \code{parallel} feature; the
biggest \emph{single line of code change} was the
\code{Int<5> $\to$ Int<2>} swap, which dropped the bench config to
a sane bit-budget.
