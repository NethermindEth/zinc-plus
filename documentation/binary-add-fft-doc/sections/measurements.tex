\section{Measurements}
\label{sec:measurements}

All numbers below come from
\code{cargo bench --bench zip\_plus\_benches --features parallel}
on Apple~M4. The corresponding test for column sizes is
\code{column\_size\_report} (and \code{\_i32} variant) in
\code{zip-plus/src/code/binary\_add\_fft.rs}, runnable as
\begin{quote}\small
  \code{cargo test -p zip-plus --release column\_size\_report -- --nocapture --ignored}
\end{quote}

\subsection{Column-opening sizes (raw and zstd-3 compressed)}

For \code{poly\_size = $2^{14}$, batch = 11}, \code{num\_rows = 4}
per polynomial, on truly-random \code{BinaryPoly<32>} inputs:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabularx}{0.95\textwidth}{l rrr}
  \toprule
  Quantity & \code{Int<2>} \code{Cw} & \code{i32} \code{Cw} & Change \\
  \midrule
  Bytes per \code{Cw} entry                & 512   & 128  & $4\times$ smaller \\
  Per-poly column (4 entries)              & 2{,}048 B  & 512 B  & $4\times$ \\
  Batched column raw (44 entries)          & 22.0 KiB   & 5.5 KiB  & $4\times$ \\
  Batched column zstd-3                    & 646 B      & 521 B    & $1.24\times$ \\
  zstd compression ratio                   & $35\times$ & $11\times$ & --- \\
  Non-zero bytes in raw column             & 703        & 703        & identical \\
  Full proof openings raw (147 openings)   & 3234 KiB   & 808 KiB    & $4\times$ \\
  Full proof openings zstd-3               & 92.7 KiB   & 74.8 KiB   & $1.24\times$ \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\noindent The striking observation is that the
\emph{non-zero byte count is identical} ($703$ bytes) for both
representations --- the actual information content of the column
is the same, and the $\code{Int<2>}$ representation simply
stored each value in a $16$-byte slot with $\sim 13$ zero bytes
of padding. zstd-3 was extracting most of that padding anyway, so
the compressed size only drops by $\sim 19\%$; the bigger wins
from the $\code{i32}$ shrink are in raw size ($4\times$),
working-set memory, cache locality, and ALU width.

\subsection{Why the lifted code is so compressible}

For a randomly-sampled $\code{BinaryPoly<32>}$ input vector at
$\code{poly\_size} = 2^{14}$, \emph{$96.9\%$} of the raw column
bytes are zero (in the \code{Int<2>} representation). Three
compounding reasons:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Heavy bit-width over-provisioning.} The
    coefficient is stored in $16$ bytes, but
    the loose worst-case after $m = 14$ FFT stages is bounded by
    $33^{14} \approx 2^{71}$; so the high $\sim 7$ of $16$ bytes
    per coefficient are always zero just from that bound.
  \item \textbf{Reality is tighter than $33^m$.} The codeword is
    a \emph{sum} of $N$ lifted-GF products
    (\autoref{sec:i32-shrink}), so the worst case is linear in
    $N$, not exponential. Empirical max at $N = 2^{14}$ is
    $\sim 2^{21}$.
  \item \textbf{Many entries are exactly zero.} The polynomial
    has $\code{row\_len} / \code{codeword\_len} = 1/4$ non-zero
    novel-basis coefficients (the zero-padding); the FFT
    preserves significant sparsity in many evaluation points.
\end{enumerate}

The $\code{i32}$ shrink removes most of the wasted high-byte
zeros at the source, so the compression ratio drops from
$35\times$ to $11\times$ --- but the absolute compressed size is
still smaller because the non-zero ``effective'' bytes are still
compressible.

\subsection{Test coverage}

The following tests in \code{zip-plus/src/code/binary\_add\_fft/}
exercise the implementation:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{basis::tests::*}: \code{Gf2\_32} arithmetic
    identities, the Cantor recurrence
    $\bb_i^2 + \bb_i = \bb_{i-1}$, basis linear independence, and
    the half-trace solver.
  \item \code{params::tests::*}: subspace polynomial
    monicity, $s_i(\bb_i) = 1$, $s_i$ vanishes on $V_i$, the
    Cantor recurrence at evaluation points.
  \item \code{ring\_ops::tests::*}: $\flift$-reduction
    correctness, the $\Rlift \to \GF(2^{32})$ commutative diagram
    for multiplication, the
    \code{mul\_by\_lifted\_gf32} contract.
  \item \code{add\_fft::tests::*}: the lifted-FFT-vs-naive
    equivalence, the
    $\Rlift \to \GF(2^{32})$ commutative diagram for the full
    FFT (both \code{Int<N>}- and \code{i128}-instantiated), FFT
    linearity, zero-padding.
  \item \code{binary\_add\_fft::linear\_code\_tests::*}: the
    \code{LinearCode<Zt>} interface smoke test, plus the
    column-size measurement tests
    (\code{\#[ignore]}-gated).
\end{itemize}

All tests pass under both \code{cargo test -p zip-plus} (sequential
default) and \code{cargo test -p zip-plus --features parallel}.
