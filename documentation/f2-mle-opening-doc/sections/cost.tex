\section{Cost}
\label{sec:cost}

We report communication and computation costs for \Cref{cons:open}.
The deployment parameters are
\[
  \Dval = 32, \quad
  \mu_\alpha = 192, \quad
  \text{REP} = 4, \quad
  t = 987,
\]
where $\mu_\alpha$ is the bit-length of the field-extension degree
and $t$ is the column-opening parameter recommended for
\texttt{RaaF2Code} at rate $1/4$
(see \texttt{zip\_plus::code::raa\_f2::recommended\_num\_column\_openings}).

\subsection{Communication}

The transmitted Step-7 data is:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item $a' \in \polyringat{\Dval + 3\mu_\alpha - 3}$ — \emph{single} polynomial,
    bit-width $\le 608$, $\le 76$ bytes;
  \item $b' \in \polyringat{\Dval + 2\mu_\alpha - 2}^{n_{\mathrm{rows}}}$ — vector of
    $n_{\mathrm{rows}}$ entries, each $\le 414$ bits, $\le 52$ bytes
    per entry; total $\le 52 \, n_{\mathrm{rows}}$ bytes;
  \item $\mathrm{cr}' \in \polyringat{\Dval + 2\mu_\alpha - 2}^{n_{\mathrm{cols}}}$ —
    same per-entry width as $b'$; total $\le 52 \, n_{\mathrm{cols}}$ bytes;
  \item per opened column $k \in \{1,\ldots,t\}$: the column data
    $\mathrm{col\_values}_k \in \cellring^{K \cdot n_{\mathrm{rows}}}$
    (which is $K \cdot n_{\mathrm{rows}} \cdot \Dval/8 = 4 K
    \, n_{\mathrm{rows}}$ bytes at $\Dval = 32$) plus the Merkle
    authentication path $\pi_k$ of length $\le \log_2(n_{\mathrm{cw}})
    \cdot 32$ bytes.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Concrete numbers}

For an opening on a matrix of shape $n_{\mathrm{rows}} =
n_{\mathrm{cols}} = 2^{8} = 256$, $K = 2$, REP$=4$ (so $n_{\mathrm{cw}}
= 1024$):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item $a'$: $76$ bytes.
  \item $b'$: $13{,}312$ bytes ($256 \times 52$).
  \item $\mathrm{cr}'$: $13{,}312$ bytes.
  \item Per opened column: $4 \cdot 2 \cdot 256 + 32 \cdot 10 =
    2{,}368$ bytes. At $t = 987$: $\approx 2.34$ MB.
\end{itemize}
Total Step-7 proof: $\approx 2.37$ MB. The Merkle-bound column openings
dominate; the lifted polynomial data is $< 1\%$ of the total.

\subsection{$\gamma$-batching savings}

The pre-$\gamma$ design would send per-column triples
$(a_g', b_g, \mathrm{cr}_g)$ for $g = 1, \ldots, K$. The $\gamma$-batch
of \Cref{sec:gamma-batch} reduces this to a single triple. Concretely:

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.2}
\begin{tabular}{l c c}
\toprule
& Per-column (un-batched) & $\gamma$-batched \\
\midrule
$a'$ (polynomial) & $K \cdot 56$ B & $76$ B \\
$b'$ (polynomial vector) & $K \cdot 32 \, n_{\mathrm{rows}}$ B & $52 \, n_{\mathrm{rows}}$ B \\
$\mathrm{cr}'$ (polynomial vector) & $K \cdot 32 \, n_{\mathrm{cols}}$ B & $52 \, n_{\mathrm{cols}}$ B \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Per-poly proof size: un-batched (per-column emission) vs
$\gamma$-batched. Per-entry widths grow with batching by a
$\gamma$-degree factor ($+192$ bits), but the count drops from
$\Theta(K)$ to $\Theta(1)$.}
\end{table}

For $K = 2$ the saving on the lifted polynomial data is roughly
$(K \cdot 32 - 52) / (K \cdot 32) \approx 19\%$ on $b'$ and $\mathrm{cr}'$.
For $K = 10$ the saving is $\approx 84\%$. The $\gamma$-batch grows
asymptotically as $1/K$ times the un-batched cost.

\subsection{Prover computation}

Per opening, the prover-side work is:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item One \texttt{AlphaPolyBasis::new}: $\sim 10^5$ word ops for
    the Gauss–Jordan inversion + $191$ $\GFt$-multiplications.
  \item $|q_0'| + |q_1'| = n_{\mathrm{rows}} + n_{\mathrm{cols}}$
    lift evaluations, each $192$ word XORs.
  \item $K + n_{\mathrm{rows}}$ lift evaluations for
    $\gamma_1, \ldots, \gamma_K$ and $c_1, \ldots, c_{n_{\mathrm{rows}}}$.
  \item Per column $g$: $n$ cell-lift operations
    ($\cellring \hookrightarrow \polyringat{1}$, free) plus
    $n_{\mathrm{rows}} \cdot n_{\mathrm{cols}}$ carryless multiplications
    of size at most $192 \times \Dval$ for $b_g$; similar count for
    $\mathrm{cr}_g$ but along the other axis.
  \item One inner-product over $\polyring$ for $a_g'$.
  \item $\gamma$-batching: $K$ multiplications and $K$ XORs into the
    accumulators $a', b', \mathrm{cr}'$.
  \item $t$ Merkle column extractions and path computations.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Verifier computation}

Per opening, the verifier-side work is:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item One \texttt{AlphaPolyBasis::new}: same as prover-side
    (the table is regenerated, not transmitted).
  \item $|q_0'| + |q_1'| + K + n_{\mathrm{rows}}$ lift evaluations.
  \item Three polynomial inner products over $\polyring$ (Checks~1, 3,
    and the LHS of Check~4).
  \item One $\psia$-evaluation of $a' \in \polyringat{608}$: $608$
    multiplications and additions in $\GFt$. With precomputed powers
    of $\alpha$ this is $O(608) \cdot O(1)$ field operations.
  \item One $\encode$ call on $\mathrm{cr}'$ — a $\polyring$-valued
    rate-$1/4$ RAA encode of $n_{\mathrm{cols}}$ width-$\le 414$ bit
    entries.
  \item Per opened column: one Merkle path verification (Blake3-cheap)
    + one $\polyring$-valued inner product of width
    $\le \Dval + 2 \mu_\alpha$.
\end{itemize}

The dominant verifier costs are the $\encode$ call (which scans
$n_{\mathrm{cw}}$ entries of width $\le 414$ bits — i.e., $\sim
n_{\mathrm{cw}} \cdot 7$ word XORs) and the $t$ column-opening checks
(each $O(K n_{\mathrm{rows}})$ word ops plus a Blake3 hash chain).

\subsection{Comparison to integer-pipeline Step 7}

The integer-pipeline Zip+ opening at the same shape carries:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item $b$: $n_{\mathrm{rows}}$ entries in $\F_q$ (32 bytes each).
    Total: $32 \, n_{\mathrm{rows}}$ B.
  \item $\mathrm{combined\_row}$: $n_{\mathrm{cols}}$ entries in
    $\Z_M$ (the wide combined-row type) of size $\sim 32$ bytes.
    Total: $32 \, n_{\mathrm{cols}}$ B.
  \item Per opened column: $K \cdot n_{\mathrm{rows}}$ entries in
    $\F_q$ ($32$ bytes each) plus a Merkle path. Per-column total:
    $32 K \, n_{\mathrm{rows}} + 32 \log_2(n_{\mathrm{cw}})$ B.
\end{itemize}

The integer-pipeline overheads per-entry are $32$ bytes per $\F_q$
element. \Cref{cons:open}'s per-entry sizes are $\le 52$ bytes (a
$1.6\times$ factor) — the cost paid for staying in $\polyring$
throughout. The Merkle-bound column data is exactly the same width
($\Dval/8 = 4$ bytes per cell in the $\F_2$ pipeline, same as the
integer pipeline at $\Dval = 32$). The dominant communication term —
column openings — is therefore size-equivalent across the two paths;
the $\F_2$ pipeline's modest overhead is in the lifted polynomial
data (the first three rows of Table~1), which is $< 1\%$ of total
size.

\subsection{Where the design is tight}

Three knobs have non-obvious cost-soundness trade-offs:

\paragraph{$t$ (column-opening parameter).} Determined entirely by the
proximity-error budget; \texttt{RaaF2Code} at REP$=4$ requires $t =
987$ for $2^{-100}$-class soundness. Reducing REP (better rate)
reduces $\delta$ and thus increases $t$ for the same soundness.

\paragraph{Cell width $\Dval$.} Doubling $\Dval$ doubles the
proof-byte budget for the column-opening section ($\Dval/8$ bytes per
cell, $K \, n_{\mathrm{rows}} \cdot t$ cells opened). The deployed
$\Dval = 32$ is chosen to fit a $\F_q$-projection target of $\le 64$
bits via a single $\GFt$-multiplication; smaller $\Dval$ would shrink
proofs but require finer constraint granularity in UAIRs.

\paragraph{Matrix shape.} The split $\nu = \nu_0 + \nu_1$ between row
and column directions affects only the lifted polynomial data
($b'$ has $n_{\mathrm{rows}}$ entries, $\mathrm{cr}'$ has
$n_{\mathrm{cols}}$ entries) — the column-opening cost is
shape-symmetric. Choosing $n_{\mathrm{rows}} \approx n_{\mathrm{cols}}
= \sqrt{n}$ minimises $b' + \mathrm{cr}'$ at a given $n$.
