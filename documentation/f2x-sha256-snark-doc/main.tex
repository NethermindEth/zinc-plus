\begin{center}
\textcolor{blue}{\textit{Companion note to the Zinc\texttt{+} paper. It
describes the end-to-end all-$\Ftwo[X]$ proof system for SHA-256: the
arithmetization, the binary-field polynomial interactive oracle
reduction, and the bit-sliced polynomial commitment. Machine-assisted
draft, under author revision.}}
\end{center}
\vspace{0.5em}

\begin{abstract}
We give a self-contained account of a succinct argument for SHA-256 that
operates \emph{natively} over the polynomial ring $\Ftwo[X]$, never
lifting the computation to the integers or to a large prime field. Each
$32$-bit machine word is a bit-polynomial in the cell ring
$\cellring = \Ftwo[X]/(X^{\wbit})$, and the three bitwise primitives of
SHA-256 become ring operations: $\XOR$ is polynomial addition (free at
the commitment layer); bit shifts and rotations are monomial
multiplications, realised as linear ``virtual'' columns; and modular
addition $\bmod\,2^{\wbit}$ is the Binius per-step full-adder identity in
the quotient $\Fshift$. The single nonlinear primitive, $\AND$ (hence
$\Ch$ and $\Maj$), is a coefficient-wise Hadamard product, which we
discharge by an oblong zerocheck whose output is tied back to the
commitment through a subspace-Lagrange projection. We then describe the
binary-field polynomial interactive oracle reduction that proves the
resulting constraint system --- ideal check, the evaluation projection
$\psia$, and sumcheck over $\K = \mathrm{GF}(2^{128})$ --- and show how
every constraint and every Hadamard discharge reduces to multilinear
evaluation claims at a common point. These claims are settled by a
hash-based polynomial commitment whose opening is \emph{bit-sliced}:
the eq-folded $\{0,1\}$ bit slices of the committed cells form a single
degree-$<\wbit$ polynomial over $\K$, from which the verifier reads off
both the evaluation claim (via $\psia$) and the Hadamard discharge
operands (via $\psiz$) at no extra commitment cost. The construction is
presented as an idealized design, with the deployed instantiation
($\wbit = 32$, $\K=\mathrm{GF}(2^{128})$, seven chained compressions)
and its engineering choices given as remarks.
\end{abstract}
