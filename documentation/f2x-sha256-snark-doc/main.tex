\begin{center}
\textcolor{blue}{\textit{Companion note to the Zinc\texttt{+} paper: an
end-to-end, all-$\Ftwo[X]$ proof system for SHA-256 --- the arithmetization,
the binary-field polynomial IOP, and the bit-sliced commitment that ties
them together. Under author revision.}}
\end{center}
\vspace{0.5em}

\begin{abstract}
We give a self-contained account of a succinct argument for SHA-256 that runs
entirely in characteristic two, over the polynomial ring $\Ftwo[X]$, never
lifting the computation to a large prime field. Each $32$-bit word is a
\emph{bit-polynomial} in the cell ring $\cellring = \Ftwo[X]/(X^{\wbit})$, and
the three bitwise primitives of SHA-256 become ring operations: $\XOR$ is
addition (free at the commitment layer); shifts and rotations --- and their
$\Ftwo$-linear combinations, the $\Sigma/\sigma$ mixing --- are monomial
multiplications, realised as \emph{virtual} columns read off the commitment
rather than committed; and modular addition is the Binius full-adder identity,
for which the cell ring lets the prover commit a \emph{single carry bit} per
addition. The one nonlinear primitive, $\AND$ (hence $\Ch$, $\Maj$), is a
coefficient-wise Hadamard product. The feature we emphasise is how the
nonlinear part is settled: a hash-based $\Ftwo[X]$ commitment whose opening
binds, per column, a single degree-$<\wbit$ object --- the \emph{bit-slice
fold} --- off which the verifier reads \emph{both} the evaluation that
discharges the linear constraints (a monomial reading at a random point)
\emph{and} the operands that discharge the $\AND$ relations (a Lagrange reading
at the \emph{same} point). The two readings share one opening at one point, so
the nonlinear constraints cost no second commitment. We compose the
arithmetization, a binary-field polynomial IOP over $\K = \mathrm{GF}(2^{128})$,
and the commitment into a knowledge-sound SNARK under Fiat--Shamir, present the
deployed instantiation ($\wbit = 32$, seven chained compressions, a rate-$1/4$
$\Ftwo$-RAA code), and briefly position the system against Binius64, from which
the carry identity and the single-$\AND$ form of $\Ch$ are adopted.
\end{abstract}
