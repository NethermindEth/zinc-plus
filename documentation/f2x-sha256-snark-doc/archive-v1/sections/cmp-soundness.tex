\section{The soundness asymmetry}
\label{sec:cmp-soundness}

A fair comparison must record that the two systems are not at the same
soundness status. \binius{} is fully sound at its $96$-bit target. \zinc{}
is sound for the $\AND$ relations and the entire linear part, but its
modular-addition carries are presently \emph{trusted} --- the one
documented gap, ``Issue-1.''

\paragraph{What is and is not bound.} \zinc{} discharges all Hadamard
relations by the ideal-membership check, then binds the discharge's operand
evaluations to the commitment by a $\psiz$ projection. This binding
reaches operands that decompose into row-shift pair-evaluations of
committed columns --- which the $\Ch$/$\Maj$ ($\AND$) operands do, so they
are bound and unconditionally sound. The Binius adder carry operands do
not: the carry word involves a committed carry-out bit and an intra-word
$\SHR$, which the bit-axis projection $\psiz$ removes, so they do not
reduce to row-shift pair-evals of primary columns. Their $\psiz$ parents
are therefore supplied trusted (honest-prover-supplied) and folded
directly into the binding. Concretely, $12$ of the $14$ deployed Hadamards
(the modular-add carries) are honest-prover-only at the AIR layer;
end-to-end soundness holds unconditionally for the $\AND$ and linear
parts but only ``assuming honest adder parents'' for the additions.

\paragraph{Closing it: the grand-product lookup adder.} The recommended
fix replaces the $12$ carry Hadamards with one multiset-membership lookup
against a public addition table. It is \emph{sound by construction} (a
prover cannot fake a lookup), so it closes Issue-1 for free while also
removing the $\times\wbit$ bit-slice discharge cost for additions. A
characteristic-two subtlety is essential here: the lookup must use a
\emph{multiplicative} grand product $\prod(\alpha - a_i) = \prod(\alpha -
t)^{m_t}$, not additive log-derivative (LogUp). Over a field of
characteristic two the additive form $\sum_i 1/(\alpha - a_i)$ is
\emph{unsound} --- even multiplicities cancel, since $1/(\alpha-a) +
1/(\alpha-a) = 0$ --- so a cheater could use an off-table value an even
number of times for free. The multiplicative product has no such
cancellation and is sound over $\mathrm{GF}(2^{128})$.

\paragraph{Bearing on the comparison.} Because closing Issue-1 costs
\zinc{} prover time it has not yet paid, \zinc{}'s moderate-$N$ prover edge
at the matched rate includes a small soundness discount in its favour (it
proves slightly less, unconditionally). Whether the edge survives a fully
sound comparison turns on whether the lookup adder is roughly
prover-neutral --- the open question flagged in \Cref{sec:ana-prover}.
\binius{} carries no such debt: it is fully sound today, so any ``who is
ahead on the prover'' claim must fold this in. The asymptotic
($\sim$$2\times$, scaling) win is more robust to the discount than the
moderate-$N$ touch.
