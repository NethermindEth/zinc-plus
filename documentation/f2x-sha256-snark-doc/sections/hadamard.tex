\section{Discharging $\AND$: The Oblong Hadamard Argument}
\label{sec:hadamard}

This section proves the nonlinear part of the UAIR: the column Hadamard
products that encode $\AND$ (\Cref{sec:and}) and the carry Hadamards of the
modular additions (\Cref{sec:add}). The argument has three movements. A
\emph{zerocheck} reduces the whole batch of Hadamard relations to a few
evaluation claims --- but evaluations under a \emph{second} projection
$\psiz$, chosen so the bit-product structure survives where it died under
$\psia$ (\Cref{rem:and-not-survive}). An \emph{oblong} (univariate-skip)
structure makes that zerocheck cheap by keeping the base-field witness in
the base field. And a \emph{binding} ties the $\psiz$-claims to the same
commitment opening that settles the linear part, so the nonlinear part adds
no opening.

\subsection{The obligation}
\label{sec:had-oblig}

Collect the $K_H$ Hadamard relations of the trace --- the three $\AND$
products per round and the carry Hadamard of each binary addition step,
$K_H = 16$ in the deployed layout --- as triples of operand columns
\[
  \bp{u}^{(k)}\had\bp{v}^{(k)} \;=\; \bp{w}^{(k)},
  \qquad k = 1,\dots,K_H,
\]
where each operand is a fixed $\Ftwo$-linear combination of committed
columns and their row-shifts (e.g.\ $\bp{u}_{ef}=\col{E}^{\downarrow1}\had\col{E}$,
or the adder operands $\bp{a}+X\bp{c}$, $\bp{b}+X\bp{c}$, $\bp{c}+X\bp{c}$
of \Cref{lem:binius}). Writing $u^{(k)}_b(x)\in\{0,1\}$ for bit $b$ of the
operand at row $x\in\{0,1\}^{\nu}$, the obligation is the system of scalar
equations
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:had-system}
  u^{(k)}_b(x)\,v^{(k)}_b(x) \;=\; w^{(k)}_b(x)
  \qquad \text{for all } k,\; b\in\{0,\dots,\wbit-1\},\; x\in\{0,1\}^{\nu}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{The second projection $\psiz$ and the zerocheck}
\label{sec:psi-z}

We reduce \eqref{eq:had-system} with two batchings and one projection.
Relations are batched by a transcript challenge $\gamma_{\mathrm{rel}}$.
\emph{Bit positions} are collapsed by the second cell projection of
\Cref{def:projection}: identify the $\wbit$ bit positions with an
$\Ftwo$-affine subspace $H\subset\K$ of dimension $\mu=\log_2\wbit$ (so a
bit-indexed vector is the evaluation table over $H$ of a univariate of
degree $<\wbit$), let $\{L_b\}$ be the Lagrange basis of $H$, and for a
transcript point $z\in\K$ define
\[
  \psiz(\bp{w}) \;=\; \sum_{b} L_b(z)\,w_b \;\in\;\K
\]
--- the cell projection with subspace-Lagrange weights $\xi_b = L_b(z)$, the
exact analogue of $\psia$'s monomial weights $\xi_b=\alpha^b$. The
zerocheck claim is then
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:had-zerocheck}
  \sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^{\nu}} \eq(x;r)
  \sum_{k} \gamma_{\mathrm{rel}}^{\,k}
  \underbrace{\sum_{b} L_b(z)\bigl(u^{(k)}_b(x)v^{(k)}_b(x) - w^{(k)}_b(x)\bigr)}_{=\;\psiz(\bp{u}^{(k)}\had\bp{v}^{(k)} - \bp{w}^{(k)})(x)}
  \;=\; 0 .
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[$\psiz$ reduces the Hadamard system]
\label{lem:psi-z-sound}
If \eqref{eq:had-system} holds then \eqref{eq:had-zerocheck} holds for
every $z,\gamma_{\mathrm{rel}},r$. Conversely, if some equation of
\eqref{eq:had-system} fails, then over uniform $z$,
$\gamma_{\mathrm{rel}}$, $r$ the claim \eqref{eq:had-zerocheck} fails
except with probability at most $\frac{\wbit-1}{|\K|}+\frac{K_H-1}{|\K|}+\frac{\nu}{|\K|}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Idea]
Honest data makes every inner summand zero, so the claim holds identically.
For the converse, a failed equation makes the inner bit-residual
$\bp{u}^{(k)}\had\bp{v}^{(k)}-\bp{w}^{(k)}$ a nonzero bit-vector at some row;
$\psiz$ of a nonzero $\wbit$-bit vector is a nonzero degree-$<\wbit$
univariate in $z$, nonzero with probability $\ge1-\frac{\wbit-1}{|\K|}$
(Schwartz--Zippel); the relation batch by $\gamma_{\mathrm{rel}}$ and the
$\eq(\cdot;r)$ zerocheck add the remaining two terms by the standard
arguments. The $\psiz$ bound is identical to the $\psia$ bound
$\frac{\wbit-1}{|\K|}$, the two projections differing only in their weight
vector.
\end{proof}

The point of $\psiz$ over $\psia$ is exactly \Cref{rem:and-not-survive}:
the inner sum $\sum_b L_b(z)\,u_b v_b$ keeps the bit-products $u_b v_b$
\emph{separated by bit position}, so the verifier's final check is a genuine
product of per-position bits, not the scrambled cross-terms a $\psia$
projection of $\bp{u}\had\bp{v}$ would produce.

\subsection{The oblong (univariate-skip) zerocheck}
\label{sec:oblong}

Run naively, \eqref{eq:had-zerocheck} is a sumcheck over $\mu+\nu$
variables --- the $\mu$ bit-index variables and the $\nu$ row variables ---
entirely in $\K$, wasting the fact that the witness bits are $\Ftwo$-valued.
The \emph{oblong} structure (a univariate skip, in the small-characteristic
sumcheck tradition) fuses the $\mu$ bit variables into a single univariate
round.

\paragraph{Phase 1 (the bit skip).} For fixed row $x$, the operand
$U^{(k)}(Z,x)=\sum_b u^{(k)}_b(x)L_b(Z)$ is the degree-$<\wbit$ univariate
interpolating the operand's bits over $H$; likewise $V,W$. The prover sends
the univariate
\[
  R_0(Z) \;=\; \sum_{x}\eq(x;r)\sum_k\gamma_{\mathrm{rel}}^{\,k}
  \bigl(U^{(k)}(Z,x)\,V^{(k)}(Z,x) - W^{(k)}(Z,x)\bigr),
  \qquad \deg R_0 \le 2(\wbit-1).
\]
Two economies make this cheap. On the \emph{base} domain $Z\in H$ (the
actual bit positions), $U,V,W$ take the $\{0,1\}$ witness bits and
$U V - W = u_b v_b - w_b$ \emph{vanishes for honest data} by
\eqref{eq:had-system} --- so $R_0$ is zero on all $\wbit$ base points, and
the prover transmits only its values on the $\wbit-1$ \emph{off-subspace}
points. And the dominant accumulation over the $2^{\nu}$ rows, restricted
to the base domain, is $\Ftwo$ (bit) arithmetic; only the off-subspace
lift and the final fold touch $\K$. The verifier samples one challenge
$z\in\K$, binding all $\mu$ bit variables at once; $z$ is precisely the
$\psiz$ parameter, and the round's soundness cost is
$\deg R_0/|\K|\le 2(\wbit-1)/|\K|$.

\paragraph{Phase 2 (the row sumcheck).} With the bits collapsed at $z$,
the operands become row-MLEs $U^{(k)}(z,\cdot)=\psiz(\bp{u}^{(k)})(\cdot)$,
and \eqref{eq:had-zerocheck} is a degree-$2$ zerocheck over the $\nu$ row
variables. A standard sumcheck (in the eq-factored ``MLE-check'' form, which
ships two coefficients per round rather than folding an $\eq$ table)
reduces it to a discharge row point $r_H\in\K^{\nu}$ and the closing
operand evaluations
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:operand-evals}
  \psiz\bigl(\bp{u}^{(k)}\bigr)(r_H),\quad
  \psiz\bigl(\bp{v}^{(k)}\bigr)(r_H),\quad
  \psiz\bigl(\bp{w}^{(k)}\bigr)(r_H),
\end{equation}
whose combination $\sum_k\eq(k;\gamma_{\mathrm{rel}})\,(a^{(k)}b^{(k)}-c^{(k)})$
the verifier checks against the reduced claim. The total soundness error of
the zerocheck is $\frac{2(\wbit-1)}{|\K|}$ (Phase 1) plus the standard
degree-$2$ sumcheck terms, all negligible at $|\K|=2^{128}$.

\begin{remark}[The GF$(2^8)$ acceleration]
\label{rem:gf8}
The base-domain accumulation can be pushed below even $\Ftwo$-vs-$\K$ by
running the additive NTT that evaluates $R_0$ over an \emph{embedded}
$\mathrm{GF}(2^8)$ subspace with a byte-lookup table, rather than over the
monomial subspace of $\K$ directly. This is a pure performance variant: the
embedding $\mathrm{GF}(2^8)\hookrightarrow\K$ is a field homomorphism, so
the round message computed in $\mathrm{GF}(2^8)$ equals its image in $\K$,
and the soundness of \Cref{lem:psi-z-sound} is unchanged. In the deployed
prover it is several-fold faster than the monomial path and carries the
bulk of the Hadamard-discharge speedup.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Binding the discharge to the commitment}
\label{sec:had-binding}

The zerocheck has reduced the Hadamard system to the operand evaluations
\eqref{eq:operand-evals} at $r_H$. Two facts let these ride the \emph{same}
opening that settles the linear part, with no second commitment.

\emph{Operands are $\Ftwo$-linear in columns.} Each operand is an $\XOR$ of
committed-column row-shifts, and $\psiz$ is $\Ftwo$-linear
(\Cref{def:projection}), so $\psiz(\bp{u}^{(k)})(r_H)$ is the $\Ftwo$-sum of
the per-column \emph{pair-evals} $\psiz(\col{c}^{\downarrow\Delta})(r_H)$ of
its constituent columns. The discharge therefore needs only these pair-evals,
exactly as the linear path needs the $\psia$ pair-evals --- the two reuse the
same row-shift machinery.

\emph{Pair-evals fold into the multipoint eval.} The
$\psiz(\col{c}^{\downarrow\Delta})(r_H)$ are appended to the main multipoint
evaluation (\Cref{sec:multipoint}) as pointed-shift claims over the
$\psiz$-projected columns, and reduced --- alongside the $\psia$ claims --- to
the single point $r_0$. There the commitment opening binds, for each column,
the entire bit-slice fold $a'_g$ (a degree-$<\wbit$ polynomial over $\K$ whose
$b$-th coefficient is the $b$-th bit slice folded to $r_0$; see
\Cref{sec:commitment}). Because one bound object carries all bit slices, the
verifier reads \emph{both} projections off it:
\[
  \psia(a'_g) = \mle{\psia(w_g)}(r_0)\ \ (\text{linear claim}),
  \qquad
  \psiz(a'_g) = \mle{\psiz(w_g)}(r_0)\ \ (\text{discharge claim}).
\]
Concretely, the open exposes its column-batching challenge $\gamma$, and the
single binding check
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:psi-z-binding}
  \psiz(a') \;\stackrel{?}{=}\; \sum_{g} \gamma_g\cdot\mle{\psiz(w_g)}(r_0),
  \qquad a' = \sum_g\gamma_g a'_g,
\end{equation}
ties the discharge's $\psiz$ evaluations to the committed bit slices.
A final \emph{tie} recombines the now-bound pair-evals into the operand
evaluations \eqref{eq:operand-evals} (by the $\Ftwo$-linearity above) and
checks them against the zerocheck's closing claim. This is the entire cost
of binding: one extra $\K$-projection \eqref{eq:psi-z-binding} of the
already-bound $a'$, no new opening.

\begin{remark}[Adder operands are trusted]
\label{rem:adder-trusted}
The $\AND$ operands decompose cleanly into column row-shift pair-evals and
are bound as above. The Binius \emph{adder} operands do not: the carry word
involves the committed carry-out bit $\beta$ and a $\SHR^1$, which do not
reduce to row-shift pair-evals of primary columns. Their $\psiz$ parents are
therefore shipped \emph{trusted} (honest-prover-supplied) and folded directly
into the tie. This matches the soundness of the predecessor ``fused'' $\AND$
discharge exactly; committing $\beta$ and giving the adder carry a fully
sound row/bit-shift binding is the one remaining gap, a recorded follow-up.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Summary}
\label{sec:had-summary}

The Hadamard part of SHA-256 --- its only nonlinearity --- is proved by a
single batched oblong zerocheck (\Cref{eq:had-zerocheck}) whose
soundness is $O\!\bigl((\wbit + K_H + \nu)/|\K|\bigr)$
(\Cref{lem:psi-z-sound}), made cheap by skipping the bit dimension into one
univariate round over the base-field witness (\Cref{sec:oblong}), and bound
to the commitment by the single projection check \eqref{eq:psi-z-binding} on
the shared bit-slice opening. The discharge thus costs, at the commitment
layer, nothing beyond reading a second functional off the one object the
opening already binds --- the property the next section's commitment is
built to provide.
