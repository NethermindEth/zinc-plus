\section{The Binary-Field Polynomial IOP}
\label{sec:piop}

The arithmetization of \Cref{sec:arith} hands us a UAIR whose constraints
split into a linear part (ideal-membership constraints) and a nonlinear
part (column Hadamard products). This section proves the linear part. It
is the binary-field specialisation of the Zinc\texttt{+} polynomial
interactive oracle reduction: a commitment, an ideal check, an evaluation
projection $\psia$, and a sumcheck, composing to reduce ``all
ideal-membership constraints hold at all rows'' to a batch of
multilinear-extension evaluation claims at a common point. The Hadamard
part is deferred to \Cref{sec:hadamard}; both meet at the commitment in
\Cref{sec:commitment}.

\subsection{Pipeline}
\label{sec:pipeline}

The reduction runs on one Fiat--Shamir transcript through the stages
\[
  \underbrace{\text{commit}}_{\text{Step 0}}
  \;\to\;
  \underbrace{\text{ideal check}}_{\text{Step 2}}
  \;\to\;
  \underbrace{\psia}_{\text{Step 3}}
  \;\to\;
  \underbrace{\text{sumcheck}}_{\text{Step 4}}
  \;\to\;
  \underbrace{\text{multipoint eval}}_{\to\, r_0}
  \;\to\;
  \underbrace{\text{open}}_{\text{Step 7}},
\]
with the Hadamard discharge of \Cref{sec:hadamard} running alongside Steps
2--4 and folding into the multipoint eval. \emph{Step 1 is empty:} the
general Zinc\texttt{+} compiler begins by projecting $\Q[X]$ or $\Z[X]$ to
a prime field $\F_{q_0}[X]$, but the trace here is already over
$\Ftwo[X]\subset\K[X]$, so there is no prime to sample and no projection
to perform. This vacancy is the first concrete saving of the all-$\Ftwo$
choice: the per-row coefficient reduction that the integer pipeline pays
at Step 1 simply does not occur.

\subsection{Step 0: commitment, and Step 1's vacancy}
\label{sec:commit-step}

The prover commits to the primary witness columns with the hash-based
$\Ftwo[X]$ commitment of \Cref{sec:commitment}: each column is arranged as
a matrix, its rows encoded by an $\Ftwo$-linear code that does not widen
$\cellring$, and the codewords Merkle-hashed into a single root that is
absorbed into the transcript. Public columns are not committed (the
verifier evaluates their MLEs locally); virtual columns are not committed
(they are reconstructed from their sources). The commitment is binding;
its evaluation argument is \Cref{sec:commitment}. For now it is a black
box that will, at the end, certify the evaluation claims this section
produces.

\subsection{Step 2: the ideal check}
\label{sec:ideal-check}

The linear constraints are ideal-membership assertions: for each linear
constraint family $\ell$ with generator $g_\ell\in\{X^{\wbit}-1,\,X,\,0\}$
(rotation, Binius LSB, equality/boundary), and each row $i$, a fixed
$\Ftwo[X]$-combination $Q_\ell(T_i)$ of cells and row-shifts must satisfy
$Q_\ell(T_i)\in\ideal{g_\ell}$. The ideal check batches \emph{all} of
these --- across families and across all $N_{\mathrm{rows}}$ rows --- into a
single claim, following the ideal-batching stage of the Zinc\texttt{+}
compiler. It draws a row-evaluation point $\rho\in\K^{\nu}$ and a
constraint-batching randomness from the transcript, and forms, per
family, the combined polynomial
\[
  E_\ell \;=\; \sum_{i\in\{0,1\}^{\nu}} \eq_i(\rho)\,Q_\ell(T_i)\;\in\;\K[X]
\]
(an element of $\K[X]$ once the $\eq$-weights are drawn from $\K$),
together with the obligation $E_\ell\in\ideal{g_\ell}$. Because $\ideal{g_\ell}$
is an $\K[X]$-submodule, honest data --- with every $Q_\ell(T_i)\in\ideal{g_\ell}$
--- yields $E_\ell\in\ideal{g_\ell}$; a single cheating row is caught, with
the usual MLE soundness, by the random $\rho$.

\begin{remark}[The $\Ftwo$-native linear ideal check]
\label{rem:native-ic}
Every SHA-256 constraint is degree one in the trace cells, so the ideal
check runs in its linear lane: the combined polynomials $E_\ell$ are
$\Ftwo$-linear images of the committed cells, and the prover assembles
them by evaluating each constraint's bit-MLEs at $\rho$ directly over $\K$,
with no general polynomial-product machinery. The membership obligations
specialise to: $E_\ell$ has zero constant term (for $g_\ell=X$), $E_\ell$
vanishes under the rotation quotient (for $g_\ell=X^{\wbit}-1$), or
$E_\ell=0$ outright (for $g_\ell=0$).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Step 3: the evaluation projection $\psia$}
\label{sec:psi-alpha}

The combined claim still lives in $\K[X]$ --- it speaks of the cell
variable. The evaluation projection collapses it to $\K$. The prover draws
$\alpha\in\K$ from the transcript and applies $\psia$ (\Cref{def:projection}
with weights $\alpha^b$, i.e.\ substitution $X=\alpha$) to every cell of
the trace, producing the projected columns
$\mle{\psia(w_g)}\in\K[Y_1,\dots,Y_\nu]$, and likewise evaluates each
combined polynomial $E_\ell$ at $\alpha$. The reason this is sound for the
linear part is the following commutation, the formal content of
``ideal-membership constraints survive $\psia$''.

\begin{lemma}[Linear constraints survive $\psia$]
\label{lem:linear-survives}
Let $Q$ be a fixed $\Ftwo[X]$-linear combination of cells (and row-shifts)
and $g\in\Ftwo[X]$. For any trace $T$ and any $\alpha\in\K$:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em,label=(\roman*)]
  \item $\psia\bigl(Q(T)\bigr) = Q\bigl(\psia(T)\bigr)$, where on the right
    $\psia$ is applied cellwise and $Q$ is read with its $\Ftwo$
    coefficients in $\K$; and
  \item if $Q(T)\in\ideal{g}$ then $\psia(Q(T)) = g(\alpha)\cdot u$ for
    some $u\in\K$; in particular if $g(\alpha)=0$ then $\psia(Q(T))=0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) $\psia$ is $\Ftwo$-linear (\Cref{def:projection}) and $Q$ has $\Ftwo$
coefficients, so $\psia$ commutes with the linear combination; row-shifts
are positional and unaffected. (ii) $Q(T)=g\cdot h$ for some $h\in\Ftwo[X]$,
and $\psia$ is multiplicative on the relevant product (it is evaluation at
$\alpha$), so $\psia(Q(T)) = g(\alpha)\,\psia(h)$.
\end{proof}

Part (i) is what makes the projected trace a faithful target for the
sumcheck: the projection of a constraint among cells is the same
constraint among the projected columns. Part (ii) is how the membership
obligations are discharged downstream --- a rotation constraint
($g=X^{\wbit}-1$) becomes a $\K$-identity once $\alpha$ is fixed, an
LSB constraint ($g=X$) leaves a controlled $\alpha$-multiple, and an
equality ($g=0$) becomes an exact $\K$-equality.

\begin{remark}[Why $\AND$ does \emph{not} survive]
\label{rem:and-not-survive}
\Cref{lem:linear-survives}(i) fails for the Hadamard product: $\psia$ is
an algebra map for the \emph{polynomial} product, but the Hadamard product
is coefficient-wise, and $\psia(\bp{u}\had\bp{v}) = \sum_b u_b v_b\alpha^b$
is not $\psia(\bp{u})\cdot\psia(\bp{v}) = \sum_{b,b'} u_b v_{b'}\alpha^{b+b'}$.
A product of projections is not the projection of a Hadamard product. This
is precisely why the Hadamard constraints cannot ride this pipeline and
need the separate argument of \Cref{sec:hadamard}, which projects with a
\emph{different} functional $\psiz$ chosen so that the bit-product
structure is exposed rather than scrambled.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Step 4: the sumcheck}
\label{sec:sumcheck}

After projection the linear obligations are a zerocheck: a single batched
constraint MLE must vanish on the hypercube, weighted by $\eq(\cdot;\rho)$.
Concretely the prover batches the projected columns by a transcript
challenge $\gamma\in\K$ into $\mle{C}(x)=\sum_g \gamma^{g}\,\mle{\psia(w_g)}(x)$
(over primary and virtual columns), and the claim
\[
  \sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^{\nu}} \eq(x;\rho)\,\mle{C}(x) \;=\; 0
\]
encodes, by \Cref{lem:linear-survives}, that all batched linear
constraints hold. This is a degree-$2$ statement (the product
$\eq\cdot\mle{C}$), and a standard sumcheck reduces it in $\nu$ rounds to a
single evaluation point $r^*\in\K^{\nu}$ together with the per-column claims
\[
  a_g \;=\; \mle{\psia(w_g)}(r^*)\;\in\;\K,
  \qquad g = 1,\dots,K.
\]
The first round runs over the $\Ftwo$-valued (projected-from-$\{0,1\}$)
data on a fast path; later rounds are ordinary $\K$ arithmetic.

\subsection{Multipoint evaluation: a single point $r_0$}
\label{sec:multipoint}

The sumcheck leaves claims at $r^*$, but the AIR reads several previous
rows (the shift-register layout of \Cref{sec:air}), so the verifier also
owes \emph{pointed-shift} claims: evaluations of a source column at a point
shifted by a row offset $\Delta$. And --- looking ahead --- the Hadamard
discharge of \Cref{sec:hadamard} produces its own claims, the
$\psiz$-projections of the operand columns at its row point. A multipoint
evaluation argument collects all of these and reduces them to one point.
Formally, given claims at $r^*$ and a list of pointed-shift claims
$\{(\text{point},\Delta,\text{source})\}$, a single sumcheck produces a
common point $r_0\in\K^{\nu}$ at which every involved column (the
$\psia$-projected trace and, for the Hadamard path, the $\psiz$-projected
trace appended to it) carries one evaluation claim. The commitment then
opens every column at the single $r_0$.

\subsection{What the PIOP proves, and what is left}
\label{sec:piop-handoff}

At the end of Steps 2--4 and the multipoint eval, the verifier holds:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item the commitment root and the projection point $\alpha$;
  \item the common point $r_0$; and
  \item evaluation claims at $r_0$ for each committed column, both its
    $\psia$-image (the linear-constraint claims) and, when the Hadamard
    path runs, its $\psiz$-image.
\end{itemize}
These are exactly the inputs the commitment's evaluation argument expects.
By \Cref{lem:linear-survives}, certifying the $\psia$-claims against the
commitment certifies the entire linear part of the UAIR --- the rotations,
the message schedule, the modular-addition LSBs, and the boundaries. What
remains uncertified is the nonlinear part: the $16$ column Hadamard
products of \Cref{sec:air}. The next section proves those, and arranges its
claims to be exactly the $\psiz$-images at $r_0$ that the same commitment
opening will bind --- so the nonlinear part costs no second opening.
