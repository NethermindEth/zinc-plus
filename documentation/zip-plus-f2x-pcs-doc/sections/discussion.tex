\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Relation to the integer pipeline}

The integer Zip\texttt{+} opening certifies $\Z[X]$-typed witnesses by
lifting MLE claims through $\Z \to \F_q$ and running the proximity test
modulo $q$, exploiting that the encoder commutes with the reduction. The
bit-sliced opening is the structural analogue with three substitutions:
the reduction $n \mapsto n\bmod q$ becomes the evaluation
$w \mapsto w(\alpha)$; the prime field $\F_q$ becomes the binary
extension $\K$; and ``commutes with reduction'' becomes ``commutes with
the $\Ftwo$-linear, scalar-extendable encoder'' (\Cref{lem:linearity}).
The decisive difference is that $w \mapsto w(\alpha)$ is not a ring
homomorphism that fixes the canonical representative --- it is merely
$\Ftwo$-linear --- so the claim cannot be discharged by re-reducing a
single committed scalar. The bit-sliced fold restores a clean discharge
by carrying \emph{all} $\Dval$ bit-slice folds as the $\Dval$
coefficients of $a' \in \Kpoly$ and projecting once at the end.

The practical payoff over the integer pipeline is that the all-$\Ftwo$
trace pays no $\F_q[X]\to\F_q$ projection per row at commit time, and the
codewords never widen the cell ring (\Cref{lem:no-widen}); the price is
paid once, in the opening, by the $\Dval$-fold inflation of the algebraic
vectors $b'$ and $\mathrm{cr}'$ (each entry is $\Dval$ field elements
rather than one).

\subsection{Relation to the $\alpha$-lift predecessor}

The scheme's earlier opening (the ``$\alpha$-lift'', described in the
companion note on discharging GF-MLE claims) lifted the $\K$-valued
evaluation weights into $\Ftwo[X]$ through the unique inverse of $\psia$,
computed by a $\Kbits\times\Kbits$ Gauss--Jordan inversion over $\Ftwo$,
and ran the whole proximity test in unreduced $\Ftwo[X]$-arithmetic. Its
$a',b',\mathrm{cr}'$ were single $\Ftwo[X]$ polynomials of degree
$\approx \Dval + 2\Kbits$, and discharge was one evaluation at $\alpha$.
The bit-sliced opening of this note supersedes it (codebase commit
\code{b840839}). Three differences:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \emph{No matrix inversion, no abort.} The $\alpha$-lift built a
    per-$\alpha$ inverse-lift table and aborted-and-resampled on the
    $2^{-95}$ event that $\{1,\alpha,\dots,\alpha^{\Kbits-1}\}$ failed to
    span $\K$. The bit-sliced open does neither: it keeps the weights in
    $\K$ and folds the cell bits, so the lift table disappears and
    completeness is unconditional in $\alpha$ (\Cref{thm:completeness}).
  \item \emph{A field-independent claim object.} The committed claim $a'$
    has exactly $\Dval = 32$ coefficients, independent of $\Kbits$,
    whereas the $\alpha$-lift's $a'$ grew with the field extension degree.
  \item \emph{Multi-functional binding} (next subsection) --- the
    property that actually motivated the rewrite.
\end{enumerate}

The trade-off runs the other way on proof size: the $\alpha$-lift's
$b',\mathrm{cr}'$ entries were single $\Ftwo[X]$ polynomials
($\approx \Dval+2\Kbits$ bits, tens of bytes), while the bit-sliced
entries are full $\Kpoly$ elements ($\Dval$ field coefficients, hundreds
of bytes). The rewrite accepts the larger algebraic vectors in exchange
for eliminating a second, separate opening for the Hadamard discharge ---
a net win whenever that discharge is needed, which in the host protocol
it always is.

\subsection{Multi-functional openings}
\label{sec:multifunctional}

The bit-sliced opening's distinctive feature is that the single bound
object $a'$ determines not one but \emph{every} $\Ftwo$-linear functional
of the committed cells, folded along the eq-tensor.

\begin{proposition}[Functional read-off]
\label{prop:readoff}
Let $\varphi : \cellring \to \K$ be any $\Ftwo$-linear functional, with
weights $(\varphi_b)_b$ in $\K$ so that $\varphi(w) = \sum_b w_b\,\varphi_b$.
Extend it to columns by
$\Phi_g := \sum_{(i,j)} q_0[i]q_1[j]\,\varphi(\widehat M_{w_g}[i,j])$.
Then for the bound, $\gamma$-batched claim $a'$ of an accepting
transcript,
\[
  \sum_{b} a'_b\,\varphi_b \;=\; \sum_{g} \gamma_g\,\Phi_g.
\]
In particular a verifier holding $a'$ can certify the batched value of
$\varphi$ on the committed witness with \emph{no} additional commitment,
opening, or column query --- only a length-$\Dval$ inner product against
the weight vector $(\varphi_0,\dots,\varphi_{\Dval-1})$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By \Cref{thm:soundness}, $a' = \sum_g \gamma_g\,\widehat a_g'$ with
$\widehat a_g'[b] = \sum_{(i,j)} q_0[i]q_1[j]\,(\widehat M_{w_g}[i,j])_b$.
Then $\sum_b a'_b\varphi_b = \sum_g \gamma_g \sum_b
\widehat a_g'[b]\varphi_b = \sum_g \gamma_g \sum_{(i,j)} q_0[i]q_1[j]
\sum_b (\widehat M_{w_g}[i,j])_b\varphi_b = \sum_g \gamma_g\,\Phi_g$,
using $\sum_b (\cdot)_b\varphi_b = \varphi(\cdot)$.
\end{proof}

The construction's \ref{chk:discharge} is the instance $\varphi = \psia$,
$\varphi_b = \alpha^b$: the monomial-power weights, giving the main MLE
evaluation claims. The host protocol uses a second instance,
$\varphi = \psiz$, whose weights $\varphi_b = L_b(z)$ are a
subspace-Lagrange basis evaluated at a challenge $z$. This $\psiz$
read-off discharges a column-level \emph{Hadamard-product} relation --- the
binary $\mathrm{AND}$ of two trace columns, which the
$\Ftwo[X]$ arithmetization cannot express linearly --- by binding the
product's operand evaluations to the same $a'$ via the check
$\psiz(a') = \sum_g \gamma_g\,(\text{$z$-evals})_g$. Because both
functionals ride the single bound $a'$, the AND discharge costs no extra
opening: it is a second length-$\Dval$ inner product against a different
weight vector. This is exactly the saving (one open instead of two) that
prompted the un-lifted rewrite; the codebase exposes it by returning the
batch challenges $\gamma$ from the verifier so a caller can run the
$\psiz$ tie on the already-bound $a'$.

\begin{remark}
\Cref{prop:readoff} is sound only for functionals whose weight vector is
fixed \emph{before} the prover commits $a'$, or drawn fresh from the
transcript afterwards; reusing $a'$ for an adaptively chosen $\varphi$
requires the corresponding $\sum_g\gamma_g\Phi_g$ to be independently
certified (as $\psiz$'s right-hand side is, by the zerocheck that
produces it).
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Communication.} The proof carries: the claim $a'$ (one
$\Kpoly$); the row-fold vector $b'$ ($n_{\mathrm{rows}}$ entries in
$\Kpoly$); the combined row $\mathrm{cr}'$ ($n_{\mathrm{cols}}$ entries in
$\Kpoly$); and $t$ column openings, each carrying
$\lceil K/2\rceil\cdot n_{\mathrm{rows}}$ packed cells (the paired
codeword column) plus a Merkle path of $\log_2 N$ hashes. As in all
Brakedown/Ligero-style schemes the $t$ column openings dominate, and the
proof is large (multiple megabytes at the larger trace sizes); the
$\Kpoly$ vectors $b'$ and $\mathrm{cr}'$ add the bit-sliced overhead
discussed above. Reducing this proof size --- e.g. via a WHIR-style
recursive IOPP in place of the flat proximity test --- is orthogonal,
ongoing work; the construction here optimises prover time and
binding flexibility rather than proof length.

\paragraph{Prover computation.} The dominant terms are: the per-column
bit-slice folds $b_g, a_g'$ ($O(K\cdot n)$ scatter-accumulations into
$\Kpoly$), the combined-row fold ($O(K\cdot n)$ likewise), and the $t$
Merkle column extractions. The column-major commit hint and packed
storage (\Cref{rem:storage}) keep the opening's memory access
sequential. There is no matrix inversion and no eq-tensor lift; the eq
factors stay in $\K$.

\paragraph{Verifier computation.} The verifier recomputes the eq-tensor,
re-derives $\gamma$ and $c$, runs Checks~1 and~3 (each an
$O(n_{\mathrm{rows}}+n_{\mathrm{cols}})$ inner product over $\Kpoly$),
runs \ref{chk:discharge} (one length-$\Dval$ projection in $\K$), and for each of the
$t$ opened columns verifies one Merkle path and one $\gamma$/$c$-weighted
encoding identity. Encoding $\mathrm{cr}'$ through the $\Kpoly$-extended
RAA encoder is an $O(N)$ pass of $\Ftwo$-XORs per field coefficient.

\subsection{Summary}

The bit-sliced $\Ftwo[X]$ opening is a Brakedown/Ligero IOPP made native
to binary-field witnesses by two design choices: an $\Ftwo$-linear code
that does not widen the cell ring and extends scalar-wise to $\K$, and an
evaluation argument that folds cell \emph{bits} against $\K$-valued
eq-weights to produce a fixed-degree claim $a' \in \Kpoly$ discharged by
a single $\alpha$-projection. The construction sidesteps the
ring-homomorphism shortcut of the integer pipeline and the matrix-inverse
lift of its own predecessor, keeps the committed claim small and
field-independent, and --- because it binds the full bit-slice fold ---
supports several distinct linear-functional read-offs from one opening,
which the host protocol exploits to discharge column Hadamard products at
no extra cost.
