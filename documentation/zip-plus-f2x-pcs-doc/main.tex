\vspace{0.5em}

\begin{abstract}
We describe a hash-based polynomial commitment scheme (PCS) for
multilinear polynomials whose coefficients are bit-polynomials in the
ring $\cellring = \Ftwo[X]/(X^{\Dval})$, together with an evaluation
argument that discharges the multilinear-extension evaluation claims
produced by a binary-field sumcheck. The scheme is the
$\Ftwo[X]$-native instantiation of Zip\texttt{+}, the Brakedown/Ligero-style
interactive oracle proof of proximity (IOPP) underlying Zinc\texttt{+}. Its
two pillars are an $\Ftwo$-linear Repeat--Accumulate--Accumulate code,
whose codewords never widen the cell ring, and a \emph{bit-sliced}
evaluation opening that runs the proximity test entirely inside
$\Ftwo[X]$-arithmetic while binding claims stated over the binary
extension field $\K = \mathrm{GF}(2^{\Kbits})$. The opening's central
object is, for each committed column, the degree-$<\Dval$ univariate
$a' \in \Kpoly$ whose $b$-th coefficient is the boolean-hypercube fold
of the column's $b$-th bit slice. Because the evaluation projection
$\psia : \cellring \to \K$, $w \mapsto w(\alpha)$ is $\Ftwo$-linear, the
original claim is recovered by a single coefficient projection
$a'(\alpha)$; this avoids the per-row $\K$-projection paid by the
integer pipeline and avoids the $\K$-to-$\Ftwo[X]$ basis inversion of
the scheme's earlier ``$\alpha$-lift'' variant. A transcript-fresh
batching challenge $\gamma$ folds all per-column claims into one opening
triple, making the algebraic part of the proof constant in the number
of committed columns. We give the construction in full --- commitment,
opening, and four verifier checks --- prove completeness, analyse
knowledge soundness by enumerating the prover's deviation modes, and
report the deployed parameters. A short final section observes that the
bit-sliced opening binds the entire bit-slice fold rather than a single
field projection, so a single opening can simultaneously discharge
several distinct $\Ftwo$-linear functionals of the committed cells ---
a property the host protocol uses to verify column-level Hadamard
products at no extra opening cost.
\end{abstract}
